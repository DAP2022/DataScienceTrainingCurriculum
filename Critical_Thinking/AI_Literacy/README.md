# Ai Literacy & DS Ethics

** This is a 12-hour mini-course that is part of the Jackson Laboratory Foundational Data Science Training Curriculum ** 

Here is the most recent syllabus for the July 2026 [hybrid course.](AiLiteracy_Syllabus_0326.docx) 

## Course Summary
_If Ai is just math and code, how does it learn, why does it hallucinate, and how can we harness it responsibly without losing our own ability to think?_

Ai literacy is emancipatory. Understanding the limits and benefits of popular Ai tools is a requirement for individuals to autonomously participate in all aspects of modern society. This 12.5-hour course is a code-free exploration of Ai tools, with an emphasis on broadly understanding and interacting with different types of Machine Learning and Large Language Models. Along with an introduction to the foundational concepts, we will also hear JAX speakers explain how they are practically incorporating Ai/LLM to optimize their own research.


## Course Learning Outcomes  
**1. Day 1 – Foundations of AI, ML & Data Science**
 -	Distinguish Ai literacy (understanding what Ai is) from Ai fluency (engaging with Ai effectively, efficiently, ethically, and safely).
 -	Distinguish “Ai”, “machine learning” and “data-science / statistics” and recognize where supervised vs. unsupervised learning fit in the conceptual network. 
 -	Trace key milestones in the history of Ai to appreciate that the field isn’t “new”.
 -	Explain core prediction tools already familiar in science (logistic regression, K-means) and where simple neural-network models extend them.
 -	Discuss the assumptions that are incorporated into these models at each step in the process and how they can impact the output.
 -	Identify which tasks in their own work are strong Ai delegation candidates and which require human judgment.

**2. Day 2 - Inside Neural Networks (FC, CNN, RNN)**
 - Explain what a perceptron is and connect it back to logistic regression from Day 1.
 - Describe how activation functions enable fully connected layers to learn non-linear patterns.
 - Explain what a convolutional layer does and when to prefer CNNs over fully connected networks.
 - Explain why recurrent networks add a loop and identify the vanishing gradient problem as their core limitation.
 - Differentiate CNNs, RNNs and when to favour each architecture & Recognize limitations of each 
 - Contrast generative vs. discriminative / predictive models; outline GAN generator–discriminator interplay.

**3. Day 3 – LLMs & Transformer architecture**
 - Explain at a high level how self-attention works and why it replaced recurrence for many tasks.
 - Decode the GPT acronym (Generative, Pre-trained, Transformer) and connect each term to concepts from Days 1–2.
 - Describe how tokenization and embeddings convert language into the numerical representations LLMs operate on.
 - Explain why LLMs are non-deterministic and what temperature controls.
 - Connect pre-training, fine-tuning, and RLHF to the alignment problem, and articulate why LLMs are optimized for plausibility, not truth.

**4. Day 4 – Prompt Engineering, Ethics, Alignment & Looking Ahead**
 - Explain why prompt structure matters architecturally; how the prompt shapes the model's probability distribution.
 - Apply the 4Ds framework to their own work context and make an initial Delegation map.
 - Construct a complete, well-structured prompt using context & Compare zero-shot, one-shot, and few-shot prompting and explain the tradeoffs between them.
 - Produce a complete, tested, peer-reviewed prompt protocol for a real task from their own work.
 - Evaluate model answers (accuracy, bias, toxicity) and understand alignment debates.

**5. Day 5 – Ethics**
 - Identify ethical frameworks (Belmont report, FAT) and articulate why evaluation matters.
 - Identify the point in an AI pipeline where algorithmic bias enters and explain why better prompting alone cannot fix it.
 - Articulate the illusion of understanding risk and identify one personal mitigation strategy
 - Produce a complete, ethics-audited personal Ai Fluency Protocol
